# Slide Patterns for Academic and Analytical Presentations

Use these patterns when building a structured professional deck.

## 1. Title Slide

Include:
- deck title framed as a statement or question
- presenter / owner
- organisation
- date or meeting context

## 2. Context Slide

Title example:
- `Mystery Shopper must move to a DMZ deployment because external users cannot use the internal network path`

Use:
- 3 focused bullets
- one small supporting visual if needed

## 3. Problem Slide

Title example:
- `Internal Entra-only authentication does not fit public external Mystery Shopper users`

Use:
- concise bullets
- maybe one risk box

## 4. Architecture Slide

Title example:
- `A separate DMZ VM keeps the public survey isolated from internal frontends`

Use:
- one architecture diagram
- short bullets on the side
- scale the diagram and side content so the slide uses the page well without leaving large dead space

## 5. Decision Options Slide

Title example:
- `Signed links, OTP, and link-plus-OTP offer different trade-offs between friction and control`

Use:
- one comparison table
- one short recommendation callout
- make the table wide enough and the supporting callout large enough to avoid an empty lower half

## 6. Deployment Slide

Title example:
- `The new Mystery deployment path can be prepared now even before the final auth decision`

Use:
- numbered steps
- simple status markers

## 7. Security Slide

Title example:
- `The DMZ survey should expose only the minimum public frontend and backend routes`

Use:
- one diagram or one checklist

## 8. Recommendation Slide

Title example:
- `Signed invitation links plus OTP are the strongest fit if invitation-only control is required`

Use:
- 2 to 4 bullets maximum

## 9. Next Steps Slide

Title example:
- `Infrastructure access, runner setup, and auth implementation are the next gated activities`

Use:
- short action list
- owners if known

## 10. Layout Density Guidance

When building professional platform or governance decks:
- prefer two-column or card-based layouts that fill the slide proportionally
- do not compress everything into the upper-left quadrant
- ensure charts, screenshots, and diagrams are large enough to carry the right side of the slide visually
- keep bottom whitespace tight by moving supporting notes and key takeaway boxes upward only when needed, not by leaving large unused bands

## 11. Conclusions Slide

This should stay on screen for Q&A.

Use:
- 2 to 4 takeaways
- final recommendation
